Hydro jetting uses high-pressure water, not a mechanical snake, to scour the entire interior wall of a pipe rather than just punch a hole through whatever is blocking it. In Mechanicsburg, it is most often called for on lines that keep re-clogging despite regular snaking.

It is a deeper, more thorough clean, which also means it is not the right tool for every clog, particularly on older or already-compromised pipe.

When It Is The Right Call.

Jetting vs. Standard Snaking

Standard Snaking.

  • Clears a localized blockage.
  • Lower cost for a first-time, single clog.
  • Leaves buildup on the pipe walls beyond the blockage itself.

Hydro Jetting.

  • Scours the full pipe interior, not just the clog point.
  • Effective against grease buildup and root intrusion.
  • Reduces how quickly the same clog returns.
  • The standard fix for a recurring or main-line clog.

Not Right For Every Pipe.

When Jetting Is The Wrong Tool

Older, already-fragile pipe, particularly cast iron with significant internal corrosion or clay pipe with existing cracks, can be damaged by full-pressure jetting. A camera inspection before jetting confirms the pipe's condition, and pressure is adjusted or a different method is used entirely if the line cannot safely handle it. This is why jetting is offered alongside inspection, not as a default first step on every service call.

A jetted line generally comes with a straightforward before-and-after comparison via camera footage, letting a homeowner see the actual pipe interior before treatment and again once it is finished. That visual confirmation is worth asking for, since it turns what would otherwise be an invisible service into documented proof the buildup that was causing the recurring clog is genuinely gone, not just pushed further down the line.

A line that has been jetted and clogs again within weeks, rather than months or years, often has a structural problem beneath the buildup, worth investigating as a possible sewer line repair rather than repeating the same cleaning.

Jetting is also a preventive option, not just a reactive one, for a household aware of an older cast- iron or clay main line with a history of slow buildup even without a full clog yet. Scheduling a preventive jetting on a known-problem line every couple of years, before it clogs completely, tends to cost less over time than repeated emergency snaking calls once the line finally backs up. It also gives a technician a natural opportunity to run a camera through the line at the same time and flag any early structural concern before it becomes an emergency. A camera pass during that same visit is also a practical time to check for early supply-line corrosion on an older property, even though it is a separate system from the drain being jetted.

Jetting pressure itself is adjustable rather than a single fixed setting, which is part of what makes it appropriate for a wider range of pipe conditions than the term "high pressure" might suggest on its own. A technician selects a pressure and nozzle type suited to the specific pipe material and diameter, using a gentler setting on older or narrower lines and a more aggressive setting on modern, larger-diameter PVC main lines with heavy grease buildup. That calibration is exactly why the camera inspection happens first, rather than treating every jetting job as an identical procedure regardless of what is actually being cleaned. A kitchen line jetted for grease buildup is also worth pairing with a look at the garbage disposal feeding it, since the two share the same root cause.
