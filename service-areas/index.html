Service Areas.

Coverage runs across Mechanicsburg borough and the surrounding townships and boroughs that share its water supply, housing stock, and daily commute patterns. Each area below has its own real local page covering the specific housing era, geography, and plumbing issues that actually come up there, rather than a single generic description applied to every town on the list. Not sure if your address is covered? Call (717) 349-9373 and ask directly.

The five areas below range from a tiny, densely built historic borough to a fast-growing township still under active construction, and the plumbing issues that actually come up in each one reflect that real variation rather than a single shared profile.

Upper Allen Township is seeing some of the most active new construction in the immediate area. Hampden Township carries the largest population base in the county. Silver Spring Township still has a real cluster of 18th-century limestone farmhouses on private wells. Shiremanstown is the smallest and most uniformly historic of the five, and Camp Hill holds a National Register-listed historic district just across the river from Harrisburg. Each town's own page below goes into the specific detail behind that summary.

If an address falls just outside these five named areas, it is still worth a call rather than assuming coverage does not extend that far. Service boundaries follow real township and borough lines, water supply, and daily route patterns, not a strict radius on a map, and nearby streets just across a boundary line are often served the same way as the listed area itself.

These five areas were not chosen arbitrarily. Each one borders Mechanicsburg directly or borders another area on this list, forming a connected service territory rather than a scattered list of unrelated towns. Upper Allen Township sits directly south of the borough along Yellow Breeches Creek. Hampden Township sits to the northeast and is the largest single municipality in the group. Silver Spring Township extends north from Hampden toward the Perry County line. Shiremanstown and Camp Hill sit east, bordering both Hampden Township and each other, closer to the Susquehanna River and Harrisburg. That geography is also why response times across the group tend to be consistent: a technician already working in one of these areas is rarely far from the next one over.

Coverage decisions also account for where a technician is actually likely to be at a given time of day, not just distance on a map. Daily routes are planned around this five-area cluster specifically, which is part of why same-day availability tends to hold up across all five rather than favoring whichever town happens to be closest to a central office.

A property just outside these boundaries, in a neighboring township not listed here, should still call rather than assume the answer is no. Coverage sometimes extends a short distance beyond the formally listed areas depending on the specific street and how it connects to the daily route pattern, and the only reliable way to confirm it for a specific address is to ask directly.

Each area page below also lists the real ZIP codes it covers, since municipal and postal boundaries do not always line up exactly with a township or borough line. A street address on the edge of one township sometimes carries a ZIP code more commonly associated with its neighbor, which is one more reason a quick phone call settles the question faster than guessing from an address alone.

None of this changes the price or the response time. An address just outside the five named areas that turns out to be covered gets the same upfront pricing and the same 24/7 emergency line as an address squarely in the middle of one of them.

Where We Work.

Service Areas, In Full.

Upper Allen Township

One of the most actively new-construction townships in the immediate area right now. dozens of named subdivisions (Mackenzie, Charlotte, Monroe, Southfield among them) are mid-build, alongside established mid-century neighborhoods closer to the Mechanicsburg line.

Hampden Township

A broad mix spanning the township's 1845 incorporation through decades of postwar and modern suburban growth, giving the county's largest population base by a wide margin.

Silver Spring Township

The township keeps an official historic-building list dominated by 18th- and 19th-century limestone farmhouses, alongside the newer subdivisions driving its recent growth.

Shiremanstown

The smallest and most uniformly historic housing stock of the five towns. Median home value runs noticeably lower than the newer-construction towns, reflecting an older, denser building stock rather than an active-development pattern.

Camp Hill

A National Register-listed historic district anchors the borough's older housing stock, just across the Susquehanna River from Harrisburg.
