A dripping faucet is more than an annoyance. A single steady drip can waste thousands of gallons a year.

In Mechanicsburg's hard water, the mineral content that builds up inside a cartridge or valve is usually what causes the drip in the first place. Sink leaks often start under the cabinet rather than at the faucet itself, at a supply line connection or a shutoff valve that has corroded shut, which is worth checking before assuming the faucet is the culprit.

Know The Signs.

Faucet Drip Or Something Bigger?

Steady Drip From The Spout.

Usually a worn cartridge, O-ring, or washer, one of the more straightforward faucet repairs.

Leak Under The Sink.

Often a supply line connection or shutoff valve rather than the faucet itself, worth a look before assuming.

Low Water Pressure From One Faucet.

Frequently a clogged aerator from mineral buildup, an easy fix once identified.

Handle Feels Loose Or Wobbly.

Usually a worn mounting nut beneath the sink deck, not a sign the whole faucet needs replacing.

Water Actively Leaking Under The Sink?

Shut off the valve below the sink if reachable, then call.

Repair Or Replace.

When Replacement Makes More Sense

A faucet under 8 to 10 years old with a single failed component is almost always worth repairing rather than replacing. Beyond that age, mineral scale has usually worked into enough internal parts that a second repair tends to follow within a year or two.

At that point a new fixture is the more cost-effective call, especially paired with a check of the shutoff valves underneath, which corrode on the same rough timeline as the faucet itself in hard water areas.

The kitchen faucet sees more daily cycles than any other fixture in the house, which is why it is usually the first to show mineral wear even in a home where every other faucet still works fine. A pull- down sprayer head, with its extra hose and internal check valve, adds one more failure point beyond a standard faucet, and a sprayer that will not retract properly is often a simple hose-guide issue rather than a sign the whole unit needs replacing.

Bathroom faucets fail on a slightly different timeline than kitchen faucets, mainly because they run for shorter bursts but see more exposure to soap residue and toothpaste buildup around the base. A wobbly bathroom faucet base is usually a loosened mounting nut under the sink deck rather than a valve problem. It gets mistaken for something bigger simply because reaching under a pedestal or vanity sink to tighten it is awkward, not because the repair itself is complex.

A single-handle faucet and a two-handle faucet fail through slightly different mechanisms worth knowing before assuming a fix. A single-handle unit typically relies on one cartridge controlling both temperature and flow, so a drip usually means that one part needs replacing. A two-handle faucet has a separate valve for hot and cold, which means a drip from only one side points specifically to that handle's valve rather than the whole fixture, often making the repair narrower in scope than it first appears.
