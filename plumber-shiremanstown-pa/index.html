Local History.

Shiremanstown, PA Facts.

Shiremanstown was settled 1864 (named for Daniel Shireman, a German-immigrant butcher who settled the area), with a population of 1,634 (2020 census), a tiny, dense borough at 0.31 sq mi. It borders Hampden Township to the north, Lower Allen Township to the south. The area's best-known landmark is St. John's Lutheran Church.

Shiremanstown takes its name from Daniel Shireman, a German immigrant and butcher who settled the area in the 18th century and became a prominent local figure well before the borough incorporated in 1864. At just 0.31 square miles, it is the smallest of the five towns covered here by a wide margin, and one of the most densely built, over 5,300 people per square mile. St. John's Lutheran Church anchors the small downtown grid. What that density and age actually means for plumbing is straightforward. This borough was substantially built out well before PEX or even widespread copper piping was standard. A much higher share of homes here are still running original galvanized supply lines than in the newer townships nearby. Median home values running noticeably below Upper Allen or Silver Spring's newer-construction prices reflect that same age profile, not a difference in desirability.

Borough hall sits on West Green Street, a single small municipal building serving the entire 1,600-some residents. That scale means most homes here are within a few blocks of a neighbor whose plumbing was installed in the same original building wave. A burst pipe on one street often means a call from the next street soon after, sometimes the same week, since the same original supply lines were laid down at roughly the same time across most of the borough.

Given how much of the borough still runs on original galvanized supply lines, a repiping assessment is worth getting even without a current leak. It is a simple way to know where the property actually stands before a small drip turns into a bigger, more disruptive, and more expensive repair down the road. A detection visit first confirms whether a specific complaint is isolated or part of a wider pattern.
