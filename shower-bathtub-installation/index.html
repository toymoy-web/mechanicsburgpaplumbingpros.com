Why The Plumbing Comes First.

Getting The Rough-In Right

Valve height, drain position, and waterproofing membrane installation all have to be correct before any surround, tile, or fixture goes in, since correcting a misplaced drain or valve after finish work is installed means tearing that finish work back out. A pressure-balanced or thermostatic valve, now standard in most new installations, also requires correct rough-in spacing that differs from older valve styles, worth confirming before assuming an old valve location can be reused as-is. A drain relocation for a new shower pan sometimes uncovers an aging supply branch behind the wall worth addressing in the same opening rather than closing it back up and reopening it later.

Anti-scald protection is worth confirming on any valve replacement, not just requesting it as an upgrade. Building codes in most jurisdictions now require pressure-balanced or thermostatic valves specifically to prevent sudden temperature spikes when another fixture in the house draws water elsewhere, a real safety improvement over the exposed single-handle valves common in homes built before that requirement existed. That same valve upgrade is a reasonable time to check the water heater's temperature setting, since anti-scald protection at the valve works alongside, not instead of, a correctly set tank thermostat.

A curbless or low-threshold shower entry is worth discussing during any tub-to-shower conversion aimed at long-term accessibility, since it requires a different subfloor and drain approach than a standard curbed shower pan. Getting that slope and drain placement right during the initial rough-in avoids a much larger, more disruptive correction later if a low-threshold entry becomes a priority after standard fixtures have already been installed.

Grab bar and future-proofing blocking, solid wood or plywood backing installed inside the wall cavity before the surround goes up, is a small addition worth including even for a household with no current accessibility need. Adding blocking during the initial installation costs very little compared to opening a finished wall years later to add proper support for a grab bar once it actually becomes necessary.

Bathtub weight is worth factoring into any installation plan involving an upper-floor bathroom. A cast-iron or stone-resin soaking tub filled with water and a person can add several hundred pounds of load to a section of floor framing that may not have been engineered for it. Confirming the tub material and the floor's load capacity before ordering a heavy fixture avoids discovering a structural limitation after the tub has already arrived on site. A bathroom remodel opening up floor joists for a heavy tub is also a practical time to have a nearby slow leak checked, since exposed framing makes inspection far easier than it would be once the new floor is closed up.

Common Questions.

Straight Answers

The plumbing rough-in, valve, and drain work is handled directly. Tile and surround installation is typically coordinated with a remodeling contractor, though some projects use a prefabricated surround installed as part of the same visit.

The drain position and valve height for a shower differ from a tub in most cases, and a walk-in shower pan often needs a different drain configuration than the tub it replaces.

A straightforward tub-to-shower conversion with a prefabricated surround typically takes 1 to 3 days depending on whether any unexpected issues, like subfloor damage or outdated valve rough-in, turn up once the old tub is removed. Larger custom-tile projects can run longer.
